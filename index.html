Where the city stands

The three headline numbers

Wave 1 asked every resident how the city is doing overall. These are the three measures that read the city as a whole. Each shows its full answer split, because a positive score is not a grade — the rest of the city is mostly neutral or undecided, not unhappy.

How the program ran

One program, two waves

The second wave was not a repeat. Wave 1 rated every city service and asked what matters most. Wave 2 returned to two of those subjects, city communication and transit, with concrete proposals instead of satisfaction scales. It also opened a subject Wave 1 never covered: an $80 million public-safety bond.

  1. Wave 1July 6–9, 2026

    Find out what is working, and what is not

    A full baseline of city services, safety, direction and priorities from 1,933 residents, mapped to all four City Commission districts.

    What came back: strong marks for safety and fire-rescue, and a clear pressure point on cost. Affordability was the lowest-rated measure in the survey, and cost of living was the issue residents named most.

  3. Wave 2July 21–24, 2026

    Go deeper, and put a new question on the table

    A shorter follow-up to 1,636 residents. 42% of them had answered Wave 1, so the two waves partly track the same people. It revisited communication and transit with specific proposals, added code enforcement, and asked about the bond for the first time.

    What came back: on the bond, this is not an argument about public safety. It is an information problem first and a cost problem second.

New in Wave 2 · asked twice in one survey

A short, factual briefing moves the bond vote 15 points

Wave 2 asked the bond question twice in the same sitting. Residents saw the ballot language and answered, then read a brief on the buildings’ condition and the household cost, and answered again. Information, not advocacy, accounts for the shift, and undecided residents break toward yes. It still falls short of the majority a bond needs.
